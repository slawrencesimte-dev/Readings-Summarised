Beyond government schemes

Private Insurance Providers

Some private insurers now build products specifically for gig and delivery workers. Here's how each one works, in plain language.

Bima Sugam — India's Insurance Marketplace
Rolling out in phases

A single IRDAI-supervised portal where you'll be able to compare, buy, manage, and claim insurance across every insurer — motor, health, life, and more — from one account. It's not a private company and not the same as Acko/Digit/BharatSure below; it's a government-regulated marketplace that sits alongside them.

Go to Bima Sugam Portal
How to access it
  1. Go to bimasugam.co.in and tap "Register".
  2. Enter your Aadhaar-linked mobile number and verify it with the OTP sent to you.
  3. Complete one-time e-KYC using your Aadhaar and PAN — no paper documents needed.
  4. You'll receive a Bima Pehchaan — a permanent digital ID linking every insurance policy you hold, across every insurer, into one account.
Terms you'll see on the portal
IRDAI — Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India, the government body that regulates every insurer and this portal itself.
e-KYC — Electronic Know Your Customer — a one-time digital identity check using Aadhaar/PAN, replacing paper documents.
Bima Pehchaan — Your permanent digital insurance ID on this portal — like the UAN for e-Shram, but for every insurance policy you hold.
PAN — Permanent Account Number — a 10-character ID issued by the Income Tax Department, used here to verify your identity.

Launched September 2025 — the portal is live for information now, but full compare/buy/claim features are still rolling out through 2026. Some options may say "coming soon."

What "Claim Settlement Ratio" means, and where these numbers come from

Claim Settlement Ratio (CSR) is the share of claims an insurer actually paid out in a year — the single most-cited "trust" number in Indian insurance. The authoritative source is IRDAI's own Annual Report and each insurer's public disclosure (Form NL-37); the figures shown here are compiled from that data via published secondary sources (cited under "src" on each entry) since IRDAI doesn't publish one live comparison table. Different sources sometimes report slightly different numbers for the same insurer (by claim count vs. claim value, or by financial year) — treat these as indicative, not exact, and always check the insurer's current disclosure before deciding. Where no figure could be confirmed, that's shown honestly rather than guessed.
